# Palette constellation

The public explorer is the integrated chapter at https://mindofaxd.com/constellation. The Worker redirects `/constellation/` and `/constellation/index.html` to this route. `compose-chapters.mjs` reads the saved snapshot below, scopes its IDs/styles/scripts, and places its content in the shared horizontal rail. Its assets remain under `/constellation/assets/`.

`site/constellation/index.html` and `site/constellation/assets/` are the published snapshot from the adjacent `perceptual-palette-drift` project. Its generator, templates, and palette data remain the source of truth. To prepare a future explorer update, rebuild there, then run `node site/sync-constellation.mjs` here (optionally passing the source project path), followed by `node site/build.mjs`. The sync copies only the generated HTML and public image/font assets, skips `.DS_Store`, and adds the portfolio's shared ambient background. It does not copy research reports, audits, metadata, PDFs, or raw originals.

Preserve the portfolio's wheel refinements when replacing this snapshot: opening a matching photograph pins the current color, and closing the preview keeps all three matches available. Touch/mouse previews return focus to the wheel without outlining the thumbnail; keyboard previews restore focus to the opened thumbnail. Clear the selection using the center's Clear color button, tapping the selected slice again, or tapping empty space inside or outside the wheel. The wheel shuffles all 150 photograph palettes and its six shapes on each visit, blending palettes every 3.2 seconds and shapes every 4.4 seconds. Selection, pause, and reduced-motion behavior still stop the animation.

These refinements now live in the source templates as well. The wheel invitation describes individual-color discovery; the map and photograph modal compare complete palettes. The research PDF is intentionally withheld.

The portfolio narrative lives separately in `site/constellation-story.html`. At build time, `compose-chapters.mjs` replaces the snapshot's “About this project” section with five numbered sections after the explorer: the question, the palette guessing experience, extraction tradeoffs, observations, and a combined map explanation / potential applications section. All five editorial headings use the same bold treatment. Existing method controls and disclosures remain intact. `site/case-studies.css` is scoped into each project's generated stylesheet, so the editorial layout does not affect other chapters.

Section 02 extends the wheel’s one-color exploration into a palette-guessing activity. Eight photographs from 2023–2025 span blues, reds, greens, pinks, warm amber, pale neutrals, and muted purple. They cycle through a monochrome view; three weighted paper strips come directly from unmodified snapshot palettes. The light-and-dark hint sits above the choices, without visible letter labels or an extra image-state caption. A correct guess or the reveal control sends the five swatches toward the print in sequence. Overlapping 1.3-second canvas dissolves restore groups of original RGB pixels as the swatches travel along smooth arcs, then hand back to the full-resolution original. Blurred mask edges settle as each region appears, without a brightness flash. The masks use nearest RGB purely for this visual effect; they are not a new CIELAB extraction or research result. The matching palette is marked, colors can be replayed, and the existing closest-palette viewer remains available. Incorrect guesses allow retry. Palette widths are the original extraction weights, not spatial regions or a reconstruction of the photograph. CIELAB links to the [CIE definition](https://cie.co.at/eilvterm/17-23-076), with Wasserstein’s order-independent distribution comparison explained in the disclosure.

`case-studies.js` runs within each composed chapter and binds only inside `.case-study`. New rounds, extraction changes, and category examples wait for image decoding before committing a photograph and its associated content; failed or stale requests preserve the current view. All three extend the map’s cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1) resizing to 700ms, keeping the outgoing image present during a crossfade. Explicit aspect-ratio windows contain absolutely positioned images; intrinsic image heights cannot overflow the paper during or after resizing. Native buttons support keyboard operation and status announcements. The color reveal and example transitions respect reduced motion and the shared pause control. Chapter departure, resizing, or hiding the document cancels temporary motion and removes the color layers; the correct original stays visible. A new round cannot inherit a completed filter animation. Sections 02, 03, and 04 retain the paper, tape, and gently rotated Polaroid treatment. Narrative disclosures use small plus/minus controls instead of horizontal rules around their labels. The map’s local motion button is hidden at composition time; reduced-motion preferences and the shared portfolio control still apply. The underlying explorer snapshot remains untouched.

The extraction comparison uses a documented follow-up experiment from the adjacent `perceptual-palette-drift` repository, not new results computed by this site. `docs/constellation/UNCLIPPED_AUDIT.md` and `unclipped-audit-results.json` record the nearest match for 9536/2025: 5546/2021 with the original extraction, 6063/2024 with extreme shadows and highlights retained. The public page labels this as one diagnostic example, not universal improvement; the explorer still uses the original snapshot data.

Section 04’s six categories and counts come from `notebooks/palette_distribution_outputs/palette_distribution_metrics.csv`. `site/study-data/palette-analysis.json` preserves the per-category image membership and three verified examples per category (both photographs for the two-image category), alongside short editorial captions. The build validates complete, unique membership of the 150-photo snapshot and checks that every example belongs to its displayed category. Unframed previous / next arrows flank the photo ID in the white Polaroid caption strip and preserve the chosen photo when returning to a category. Keyboard focus follows the same arrow into the next print, without moving focus if the visitor has already moved elsewhere. The image and its short caption start alongside the category introduction on desktop; removing the separate navigation row keeps the spread compact. Mobile uses a two-column category list followed by the print with its same caption-strip arrows. It generates counts, rounded percentages, weighted palette bars, and selectable Polaroid examples. Per-example percentage captions use rounded neutral shares or top-cluster weights from the same CSV. These rules are exploratory, threshold-dependent descriptions, not validated aesthetic judgments. The research data stays build-only: no summary JSON or analysis download is shipped. Review the membership and examples whenever syncing a new extraction or collection.
