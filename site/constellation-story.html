How five colors become a comparison

The width of each band represents its share of sampled pixels. Those pixels are grouped into five colors in CIELAB ↗, which approximates perceived color differences. The extraction filters extreme shadows and highlights when enough pixels remain, so the bar describes that retained sample.

A palette keeps the colors and their proportions, but loses their positions in the photograph. There is no meaningful “first color”: Wasserstein distance compares these weighted distributions, allowing one color’s share to match across several others.

What changed, and what didn’t

The audit kept five CIELAB colors and Transport comparison, then re-extracted palettes without brightness clipping. A paired control used the same sampled pixels; neither variant filtered skin tones.

Separately, “Matched weighted” pairs colors one-to-one. Transport allows split matches and accounts for changes in proportions. Both remain available in the map.

04 / Observations

Recurring tones.
An open interpretation.

Darker palettes and red- and blue-oriented colors recurred. A distribution analysis added a nuance: five extracted colors often describe variations of one hue, or mostly neutrals.

Patterns worth exploring. Not a definitive photographic signature.

Exploratory labels for these 150 photographs, based on palette weights and hue families. Changing the thresholds can change the labels.

How to read these categories

“Single-hue distributed” describes colors spread across several clusters but concentrated in a hue family or closely aligned hues. “Neutral dominated” requires at least 65% neutral weight, after checking for a single cluster with at least 70% weight.

The remaining categories describe balanced mixtures, two-family palettes, or dominance by one extracted cluster. Each photograph receives one label; these are descriptive rules, not validated aesthetic judgments.
